YOLO11 Coronary Artery Stenosis Detection

I used YOLO11-style object detection on coronary angiography images to mark possible artery stenosis with bounding boxes and confidence scores.

What I did

I worked with black-and-white coronary angiography images. The detector places a green box around a suspected narrowed artery area and gives a confidence score.

The workflow includes image preparation, model training, detection, and visual review. For a medical detector, accuracy alone is not enough, so the evaluation should also hold precision, recall, sensitivity, IoU, and mAP.

The portfolio loop shows five angiography examples. The visible confidence labels are in the 94.8% to 98.2% range as demonstration values for the website visual.

How the model works

  1. Prepare and label angiography images.
  2. Train or fine-tune a YOLO11 detector.
  3. Run inference on patient images.
  4. Draw bounding boxes and confidence labels.
  5. Evaluate precision, recall, IoU, mAP, and clinical review cases.
Positive result: The project shows a clear computer-vision workflow for a difficult medical image task. The visual output is easy for a reviewer to inspect.
Metric note: The confidence numbers in the short portfolio video are demonstration labels. Replace them with validated test-set metrics when publishing formal clinical results.
