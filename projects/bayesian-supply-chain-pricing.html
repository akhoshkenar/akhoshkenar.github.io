Bayesian Supply Chain Pricing & Contract Design

I used Bayesian analysis for pricing in a supply chain, combined it with inventory models, and designed contracts between supply-chain parties.

What I did

I modeled the supplier, manufacturer, distributor, retailer, and customer as connected decision parties. Price and demand are uncertain, so I did not use only one fixed demand value.

I started with prior beliefs for demand and price sensitivity. New data updates these beliefs and creates a posterior distribution. The model then selects price and order quantity using expected profit under that posterior.

I compared classical models such as EOQ and Newsvendor with modern stochastic base-stock and dynamic pricing ideas. I also modeled wholesale price, buyback, revenue-sharing, and quantity-flexibility contracts.

How the model works

  1. Define prior distributions for demand and price sensitivity.
  2. Update the model with observed demand and price data.
  3. Calculate expected profit across posterior samples.
  4. Compare inventory policies and price choices.
  5. Design contracts that improve coordination between parties.
Positive result: This project joins Bayesian learning, inventory control, pricing, and contract design. It gives a modern way to make informed supply-chain decisions when information is incomplete.
