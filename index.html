How I work. CMAN Seeks simple answers underneath complex systems. I build tools that minimize waste and support informed decisions. I hold each assumption beside the data. Some models are deterministic; others are stochastic. I define terminal states and admissible decisions when needed. I try to eliminate weak options, find a maximum or minimum, and stay confident about the method while being careful about the truth in the data. Simply put, I want analysis to land in practice. I use real operational questions, and in light of this, each new model shows what can happen before people enter a real decision.

Operating room design using agent-based simulation to reduce room obstructions

Kevin Taaffe, Yann B. Ferrand, Amin Khoshkenar, Lawrence Fredendall, Dee San, Patrick Rosopa, Anjali Joseph

Health Care Management Science 26(2), 261–27815 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

This study used real operating-room movement data to build an agent-based simulation. Staff movement was divided into OR zones, and the model tested seven design and procedure factors: room size, room shape, operating-table orientation, circulating-nurse workstation location, team size, number of doors, and procedure type. The aim was to understand how design changes can reduce movement, contacts, and room obstructions and support safer surgical work.

Proactive evaluation of an operating room prototype: A simulation-based modeling approach

Kevin Taaffe, Anjali Joseph, Amin Khoshkenar, Herminia Machry, David Allison, Scott T. Reeves, RIPCHD.OR Study Group

Journal of Patient Safety 17(8), e1833–e183914 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

This research compared operating-room design alternatives before implementation. A historical data set was created from 23 video observations of real surgical procedures, and a detailed computer simulation was built from the observed traffic flow. The model used safety and risk measures to compare room designs and identify layouts that can improve staff movement and reduce conditions linked with flow disruption.

2019

Minor flow disruptions, traffic-related factors and their effect on major flow disruptions in the operating room

Anjali Joseph, Amin Khoshkenar, Kevin M. Taaffe, Ken Catchpole, Herminia Machry, Sara Bayramzadeh, et al.

BMJ Quality & Safety 28(4), 276–28362 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

The study examined 28 surgical procedures and modeled how minor flow disruptions can grow into major disruptions. It considered staff roles, tasks, OR traffic, physical location, and environmental factors. Major disruptions increased as minor disruptions increased, especially for equipment-related events and some OR locations. Layout-related disruptions made up more than half of observed disruptions, showing that room design can affect safe and smooth work.

2019

Using a systems approach to evaluate a circulating nurse's work patterns and workflow disruptions

David M. Neyens, Sara Bayramzadeh, Ken Catchpole, Anjali Joseph, Kevin Taaffe, K. Jurewicz, Amin Khoshkenar, Dee San, et al.

Applied Ergonomics 78, 293–30049 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

This work studied circulating-nurse behavior in 25 surgeries across three operating rooms. The analysis connected nurse tasks, movement between spatial zones, and workflow disruptions. Different rooms produced different work patterns and disruption patterns. The results support a systems view: the physical layout, task needs, equipment, and staff movement should be studied together when an OR is designed or improved.

DOI10.1016/j.apergo.2018.03.017 ↗
2018

The impact of operating room layout on circulating nurse’s work patterns and flow disruptions: a behavioral mapping study

Sara Bayramzadeh, Anjali Joseph, Dee San, Amin Khoshkenar, Kevin Taaffe, Roxana Jafarifiroozabadi, David M. Neyens, et al.

HERD: Health Environments Research & Design Journal 11(3), 124–13875 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

A convenience sample of 25 surgeries was video recorded and coded for circulating-nurse activities, locations, and flow disruptions. The nurse moved through multiple zones during 91% of activities. Transitional zones accounted for 58.3% of the flow disruptions involving the circulating nurse, while 28% occurred around the OR bed. The findings show that layout and zone adjacency can strongly influence movement and workflow.

2018

The influence of traffic, area location, and other factors on operating room microbial load

Kevin Taaffe, Brandon Lee, Yann Ferrand, Lawrence Fredendall, Dee San, Cassandra Salgado, Dotan Shvorin, Amin Khoshkenar, Scott Reeves, et al.

Infection Control & Hospital Epidemiology 39(4), 391–39738 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

The study first analyzed 27 recorded procedures to identify high- and low-traffic areas. It then measured microbial load during 21 procedures in four operating rooms and two seasons. Air samplers and settle plates were used together with data on movement, door openings, temperature, humidity, and people in the room. Physical movement in an area was correlated with microbial load, supporting OR layouts and work rules that minimize unnecessary movement.

Simulation-based design and traffic flow improvements in the operating room

Amin Khoshkenar, Kevin Taaffe, Miranda Muhs, Lawrence Fredendall, Yann Ferrand, Anjali Joseph, Dee San

2017 Winter Simulation Conference (WSC), 2975–298312 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

This conference paper used simulation to study operating-room design and staff traffic. 2013

A new test of randomness for Lehmer generators based on the Manhattan distance between pairs of consecutive random numbers

Amin Khoshkenar, Hashem Mahlooji

Communications in Statistics – Simulation and Computation 42(1), 202–2147 Google Scholar citations*
Abstract + DOI
Abstract summary

This paper proposed a new randomness test for Lehmer random-number generators and studied the Marsaglia effect. Instead of the usual Euclidean distance, the method uses Manhattan distance between consecutive pairs of generated numbers. The paper derives theoretical distribution functions for overlapping and non-overlapping cases and provides a statistical way to evaluate the quality of a pseudo-random number generator.
